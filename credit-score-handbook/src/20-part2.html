India's Four Credit Information Companies

CIBIL is the household name, but every regulated lender reports to all four. Here is how they differ and why that matters to you.

The legal framework in one page

India Credit bureaus in India are called Credit Information Companies (CICs). They operate under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (CICRA), the CIC Rules and Regulations of 2006, and the RBI's directions on credit information reporting. Since 2025 those directions have been consolidated into master directions, and they were amended for weekly reporting in 2026. The essentials:

  • Four licensed CICs: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian Credit Information Company of India, Equifax Credit Information Services, and CRIF High Mark Credit Information Services.
  • Mandatory membership: every credit institution (banks, NBFCs, housing finance companies, regulated microfinance lenders, co-operative banks and others) must be a member of all four CICs and report to all of them in a standard format. In theory your data is the same at every bureau. In practice, matching errors and timing gaps mean the files differ.
  • Reporting cadence: fortnightly from 1 January 2025, and four times a month from 1 July 2026, with your CKYC number included to improve identity matching.
  • One free full report a year from each CIC, including your score. Taking it from all four, spaced three months apart, gives you a free check every quarter.

Your rights as an Indian borrower

The RBI's customer-service directions for CICs and lenders took effect in late 2024 and have since been folded into the master directions. They gave Indian consumers stronger protections than many people realise:

RightWhat it means in practice
Free annual reportOne free full credit report with score per calendar year from each CIC, through its website. The CIC must show the link prominently.
Access alertsCICs must alert you by SMS or e-mail when a lender or other specified user pulls your credit information. This is an early warning against fraud.
Pre-default noticeLenders must notify you before reporting you as a defaulter, giving you a chance to pay or dispute.
30-day resolution + compensationA complaint about inaccurate data must be resolved within 30 calendar days. The lender has 21 days to send corrected data and the CIC 9 days to update it. After that you are entitled to ₹100 per calendar day of delay, paid by whichever party caused it.
Reasons for rejectionIf your request for a correction is rejected, you must be told why.
Ombudsman accessCICs fall under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme. Unresolved complaints can be escalated through the RBI's Complaint Management System (cms.rbi.org.in).
Analyst's Note

The question I hear most often is "Which of the four is the real score?" The answer is that all of them are, because all four hold your data. What changes is which one a particular lender pulls. Before any large loan, check all four reports. More than once I have seen a home loan held up by an error at the one bureau the borrower had never looked at.

The US Big Three, and the Specialists

Three nationwide bureaus, two scoring companies, and a dozen niche agencies most people have never heard of.

Founded 1899 · Atlanta

Equifax

The oldest of the three. It began as the Retail Credit Company. Its 2017 data breach exposed the personal data of roughly 147 million Americans. That breach was a major reason free credit freezes became federal law in 2018.

US roots in TRW · Costa Mesa, CA

Experian

The world's largest bureau by revenue, headquartered in Dublin. Its US business grew out of TRW's credit-information unit. Known for consumer products such as Experian Boost, which adds utility, phone and rent payments to your Experian file.

Founded 1968 · Chicago

TransUnion

Began as a holding company for a railcar-leasing business and moved into credit data in 1969. It is also CIBIL's strategic partner in India and runs bureaus in many other countries.

Scoring companies

FICO and VantageScore

Neither is a bureau. FICO licenses models that run on each bureau's data, so you have three FICO 8 scores. VantageScore Solutions is owned jointly by the three bureaus.

Your rights under US law

  • Free weekly reports from all three bureaus at AnnualCreditReport.com. This became permanent in 2023. It is the only federally authorised source.
  • Free credit freezes and unfreezes at each bureau, by law since 2018. A freeze does not affect your score.
  • Fraud alerts: an initial alert lasts one year, and an extended alert for identity-theft victims lasts seven years. Placing it at one bureau notifies the other two.
  • Disputes: bureaus generally must investigate within 30 days, or up to 45 in some cases, and correct or delete information they cannot verify.
  • Adverse-action notices: if you are declined or offered worse terms because of your report, the lender must tell you which bureau it used, and you are entitled to a free copy of that report.

Medical debt: where things stand

In January 2025 the CFPB finalised a rule that would have removed most medical bills from credit reports. In July 2025 a federal court in Texas vacated that rule, finding the CFPB had exceeded its authority under the FCRA. The bureaus' voluntary policies still apply: paid medical collections removed, sub-$500 balances excluded, and a one-year delay before reporting. Some states have passed their own laws, but the court suggested federal law may override them. Check the current position before you rely on it.

Mortgage scoring is changing

For decades, conventional mortgages sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac required Classic FICO (FICO Score 2, 4 and 5, one per bureau) from a tri-merge report, with the lender using the middle of the three scores. In July 2025 the FHFA announced that lenders may use VantageScore 4.0 instead, and dropped the plan to move to two bureaus: tri-merge stays. FICO 10T is approved but not yet in use. Separately, FICO launched a direct-licensing programme in October 2025 that lets mortgage credit resellers calculate FICO scores without going through the bureaus, which is reshaping who earns what on each score. For borrowers the advice is the same either way: clean data across all three bureaus, and a middle score that sits safely above the pricing breakpoints (Chapter 8).

Analyst's Note

American borrowers often tell me their "credit score is 780". I ask, "Which one?" The free score from a card app is often VantageScore 3.0, while the mortgage underwriter will look at three Classic FICO scores. The gap can be 20–40 points or more. Before a mortgage, pay to see the actual mortgage versions, or ask your loan officer for the tri-merge results.

Credit Reporting Around the World

Different scales, different philosophies. One rule never changes: your history usually stays behind when you cross a border.

Every developed credit market collects repayment data, but the systems differ in three ways. Who runs the bureau (private companies, the central bank, or both). What is collected: full-file systems record good and bad behaviour, while negative-only registers record only defaults. And whether there is a single consumer-facing score.

Moving countries? Plan your credit like your visa

A good CIBIL score does not make you creditworthy in Texas, and a good FICO does not help in Dubai. Bureaus are national, and data-protection law limits sharing across borders. There are a few bridges:

  • Credit-passport services. USA Some US card issuers and landlords accept translated foreign credit reports through services such as Nova Credit, which supports several countries including India.
  • Global banks. A long relationship with a multinational bank can sometimes help you get a first card abroad, depending on the bank's policy.
  • Keep the home file alive. India NRIs should keep at least one Indian card active, with a small recurring charge on autopay, so that the Indian history keeps building. It will matter when you return or buy property in India.
Do This Now: before you relocate
  • Download full reports from every bureau in the country you are leaving, and keep PDFs for future landlords or lenders.
  • Close nothing in a hurry. Old accounts in good standing keep supporting your home-country profile.
  • In the new country, open a bank account first, then a secured or starter card within the first 30–60 days (see Chapter 11).
