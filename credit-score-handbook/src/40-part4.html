Building Credit From Zero

No history is not bad history. It is a blank page, and the first two years of entries count for a great deal.

To produce a score, a model needs enough data. FICO generally needs at least one account open for six months or more and at least one account reported in the last six months. VantageScore can score thinner files. Indian CICs typically show "NA/NH" until there is about six months of history, although some products give a 1–5 risk index to very new files. Your goal is to get through this stage cleanly with as little cost as possible.

The starter ladder

Month 0: foundations
Open a salary or savings account with a bank that also issues cards. Make sure your PAN (India) or SSN/ITIN (US), mobile number and address are identical in every record.
Month 0–1: first credit line
Get a secured card (backed by an FD in India, or by a cash deposit in the US) or a lifetime-free entry-level card. USA A credit-builder loan from a credit union is another option.
Months 1–6: quiet discipline
Put one or two small recurring bills on the card. Keep reported utilisation under 10%. Set autopay for the full statement balance. Make no other applications.
Month 6–9: first score appears
Pull your reports from every bureau. Check that the card shows up correctly with the right ownership and limit.
Months 9–18: graduate
Ask for a limit increase, or move from the secured card to an unsecured one. Add a second card only if it fits your spending.
Month 24+: a mature, thin-but-clean file
Typically 720–780+ with two cards and no missed payments. Take on instalment credit only when you genuinely need it.

Analyst's Note

The best first card is boring: lifetime-free, from your salary bank, with a modest limit. Put your phone bill on it, set autopay, and leave it alone. In my files, the young borrowers with the strongest profiles at 25 were rarely the ones who chased rewards at 21. They were the ones who never missed a payment on a card they almost forgot they had.

Mastering Utilisation and Payment Timing

The fastest lever you have. Most people use it backwards because they don't know when lenders report.

Utilisation, precisely

Utilisation = reported revolving balances ÷ total revolving limits. Models look at it overall and card by card. One card at 95% can hurt you even if your overall figure is 20%.

The statement-date secret

Most card issuers report the balance shown on your statement, not what is left after you pay on the due date. If you spend ₹1,50,000 on a ₹2,00,000 limit and pay it all on the due date, you never pay interest, but the bureau still sees 75% utilisation every month.

B. Pay most before statement₹1,50,000₹1,35,000 two days before statement; rest on due date₹15,0007.5%

Same spending, same discipline, very different signals to the bureau. Scenario B also lowers the risk of an accidental late payment.

Utilisation Tactics
  • Pay before the statement date in the months before a big application.
  • Spread spending across cards so that no single card is above about 30%.
  • Ask for limit increases every 12 months or so. Ask whether it means a hard enquiry, because many existing-customer increases do not.
  • Don't close your oldest or highest-limit card. If it has an annual fee, ask for a downgrade to a free version instead.
  • USA Don't report a total of exactly zero across all cards. FICO scores a small reported balance (e.g. 1–3% on one card) slightly better. Many people let one card report a small balance and the rest report zero.

Red Flag

A small amount left unpaid, such as an annual fee, a ₹300 late charge or a $12 subscription on a card you no longer use, can become a 30-, 60- or 90-day delinquency without your noticing. Some of the worst reports I have reviewed were caused by amounts under ₹1,000. Before you stop using a card, log in and confirm the balance is zero, and check again a month later.

Repairing a Damaged Profile

There is no shortcut, but there is a correct order. Fix things in the wrong order and you waste months.

The repair sequence

  1. Stop new damage. Bring every account current. One new 30-day late during repair undoes months of progress. Set autopay for at least the minimum on everything.
  2. Get every report and list every negative item: lender, type, amount, status, date.
  3. Dispute what is wrong (Chapter 14). Inaccurate items are the only ones you can have removed early.
  4. Resolve what is right but unresolved: overdue accounts, write-offs, collections. Pay in full where you can; negotiate where you must.
  5. Cut utilisation hard. It is the quickest visible improvement.
  6. Add positive history with a secured card if your existing accounts are closed or damaged.
  7. Let time work. Negative items lose weight as they age, provided nothing new is added.

India: "Closed" vs "Settled". The most expensive confusion in Indian credit

StatusHow you got thereHow lenders see it
ClosedYou repaid everything owed, including interest and charges.Positive or neutral. The goal for every account.
SettledThe lender accepted less than the full amount (a one-time settlement).Strongly negative. Shown on your report for years and often an automatic decline for unsecured credit.
Written-offThe lender took the loss on its books. You still owe the money.Severe. Worse than settled while it remains unpaid.
Post-WO settledA written-off account later settled for less.Better than an unpaid write-off, but still a serious negative.
Converting "Settled" to "Closed"
  • Write to the lender asking for the account to be reported as Closed on payment of the remaining (waived) amount. Many lenders agree.
  • Pay through a traceable channel. Get a No Dues Certificate / NOC that states the account is fully repaid.
  • Allow time for the lender's next submissions, then check all four reports. If any still shows "Settled", raise a dispute and attach the NOC.
  • If you are offered a settlement now, ask whether you can afford full payment in instalments instead. A restructured "Closed" usually beats a cheap "Settled".

USA: collections, charge-offs and newer models

  • Paid collections: FICO 9, FICO 10/10T and VantageScore 3.0/4.0 ignore paid collections. FICO 8 and Classic mortgage FICO do not. With VantageScore 4.0 now accepted for conventional mortgages, paying collections helps more than it used to.
  • Validation first: for a collection you do not recognise, ask the collector in writing to validate the debt (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act) before paying.
  • Old debts: the 7-year reporting clock runs from the original delinquency and is not restarted by payment. In some states, though, a payment or acknowledgment can restart the statute of limitations for a lawsuit. Know where you stand before you act.
  • Goodwill requests: a polite letter asking a creditor to remove a single, explained late payment on an otherwise perfect account sometimes works. Accuracy rules mean it is at the creditor's discretion.

"Credit repair" scams

No company, app or agent can legally remove accurate negative information. Be wary of anyone who promises a "guaranteed 100-point increase", asks for fees upfront (illegal for US credit-repair organisations under federal law), tells you to dispute everything regardless of accuracy, or offers a "new credit identity". India CICs do not authorise third parties to "clean" CIBIL records. Disputes are free and you can file them yourself.

Case File (composite): the ₹18,000 settlement

A 31-year-old engineer applied for a ₹60 lakh home loan with a CIBIL score of 702. The cause was a 2019 credit-card settlement: ₹18,000 written down to ₹9,000 during a job loss. The bank offered a higher rate tier. She paid the waived ₹9,000, obtained an NOC, disputed the status at all four CICs, and kept her other cards below 10% utilisation. Four months later her score was in the high 760s and she qualified for a better tier. On a 20-year loan, the difference was worth several lakh rupees, all for the sake of ₹9,000 she had decided not to pay.

Disputes, Errors and Your Legal Rights

Bureaus hold billions of records, and some of them are wrong. Disputing an error is your right, it costs nothing, and it works more often than people expect.

The most common errors

ErrorTypical cause
Account that isn't yoursMixed file (similar name, shared address, wrong PAN or SSN), or identity fraud
Closed loan still "active" with a balanceLender did not report the closure
Late payment you didn't makePayment posted late, a processing error, or a disputed charge
"Settled" after full paymentLender's coding error, or an old settlement offer never updated
Wrong ownership (individual vs guarantor vs joint)Data-entry error; especially common for guarantor liabilities
Duplicate accountsLoan sold or transferred and reported by both old and new lender
Enquiries you never authorisedAgent or app filing without proper consent; possible fraud
Outdated negative items USAReported beyond the 7- or 10-year limit

India Dispute process

  1. Download your full report from the CIC concerned.
  2. Raise a dispute online on that CIC's website. Select the specific item, state the correction, and attach evidence (NOC, statements, ID).
  3. The CIC forwards it to the lender. The lender has 21 days to respond with corrected data, and the CIC must finish within 30 days in total.
  4. Check the updated report. Raise the same dispute at every other CIC where the error appears.
  5. Delay beyond 30 days entitles you to ₹100 per day of compensation.
  6. Unresolved or unsatisfied? Escalate to the lender's or CIC's nodal officer, then to the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in.

USA Dispute process

  1. Get your reports from AnnualCreditReport.com.
  2. Dispute with each bureau showing the error, online or by certified mail with copies of your evidence. Consider also disputing directly with the furnisher.
  3. The bureau must investigate, usually within 30 days (45 in some cases), and send you the results in writing.
  4. Unverifiable items must be corrected or deleted. Identity-theft items can be blocked using an FTC identity-theft report.
  5. If an item is upheld but you disagree, add a consumer statement of up to 100 words.
  6. Escalate by filing a complaint at consumerfinance.gov. Wilful FCRA violations can give rise to statutory damages.

Analyst's Note

Most disputes fail for one of three reasons: the request is vague ("my score is wrong"), there is no evidence, or the person disputes at one bureau and forgets the others. Be specific, attach proof, and file at every bureau where the error appears. Keep a simple log with the date filed, reference number and outcome. When you escalate, that log is your strongest evidence.

Identity Theft, Fraud and Credit Freezes

Your credit file is valuable, and that makes it a target. Protecting it costs almost nothing.

How credit fraud usually happens

  • New-account fraud: someone opens a loan or card in your name using leaked identity documents (PAN and Aadhaar copies, SSN and date of birth).
  • Synthetic identities: fraudsters combine real and fake details to create a new "person". Your data may be part of one without your knowledge.
  • Account takeover: phishing, SIM swaps and OTP sharing give criminals control of your existing accounts.
  • India Unregulated lending apps: apps that harvest contacts and documents, then harass borrowers or misuse their identity.

India Your protection toolkit

  • Act on enquiry alerts. CICs must notify you when your report is accessed. An alert you don't recognise needs action the same day.
  • Check all four reports at least yearly, using the free reports, and use app monitoring for frequent alerts.
  • Mask and lock Aadhaar: share masked Aadhaar where possible and lock biometrics through UIDAI services.
  • Handle KYC copies carefully: write the purpose and date across photocopies, e.g. "For XYZ Bank home loan only, 17-11-2026".
  • Borrow only from regulated lenders: confirm the app belongs to an RBI-regulated entity. RBI publishes a directory of digital lending apps deployed by regulated entities.
  • Report fraud fast: National Cyber Crime helpline 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, then dispute the fraudulent account with every CIC.

USA Your protection toolkit

  • Freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. It is free, does not affect your score, and you can lift it temporarily online in minutes when you apply for credit. Consider Innovis, ChexSystems and NCTUE too.
  • Fraud alert: 1 year initially, 7 years extended for victims. One request covers all three bureaus.
  • IdentityTheft.gov: the FTC's official recovery plan and identity-theft report, which supports blocking fraudulent items.
  • Weekly free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com. Use them.
  • Lock down your phone account with a carrier PIN or port-out protection to prevent SIM swaps.
Never
  • Share an OTP, PIN, CVV or UPI PIN with anyone, including callers who claim to be from your bank, the RBI or a bureau. Real institutions do not ask for them.
  • Pay an "upfront fee" to receive a loan. Regulated lenders deduct processing fees from the disbursed amount.
  • Let an agent "file your application with five banks" using your documents.

Discovered fraud? The first 72 hours

  1. Call the lender's fraud team. Ask them to block the account and record that you dispute it. Note the reference number, date and name.
  2. Report it officially. India National Cyber Crime helpline 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, plus a police complaint where a loan was taken in your name. USA An identity-theft report at IdentityTheft.gov, and a police report if a lender asks for one.
  3. Secure your accounts. Change your passwords, enable two-factor authentication, and secure your e-mail and mobile number first. USA Freeze all three bureaus and place a fraud alert.
  4. Dispute at every bureau where the fraudulent account or enquiry appears, attaching your complaint copies.
  5. Monitor monthly for the next 12 months. Fraudsters often try again. Keep a written log of every call and letter.
Key Takeaways
  • In the US a freeze is the strongest free protection available. Freeze by default and unfreeze when you need credit.
  • India has no consumer freeze, so alerts and regular monitoring are your first line of defence.
  • Speed matters. Fraud reported within days is much easier to reverse than fraud found a year later.
